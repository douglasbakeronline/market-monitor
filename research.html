Stock research

Your stocks, or every company in the S&P 500 and FTSE 100, scored two ways. Long-term asks whether it's a quality business at a sensible price and entry point. Swing reads the chart for short-term setups. Tap a stock to see exactly why.

How the scores work
Long-term (0–100)

Weighted blend of Quality 32% (margins, return on equity, growth, debt), Value 32% (forward and trailing P/E, PEG, EV/EBITDA), Entry 26% (discount to the 52-week high, position vs the 200-day, RSI) and Analysts 10% (target upside, rating). Good entry needs 72+, a strong entry score and decent quality and value, and the stock mustn't still be in a downtrend. Stretched means the business may be fine but the price has run hot. Banks skip debt and EV ratios, which don't mean much for them.

Swing (0–100)

Starts at 50 and moves with each technical signal: trend (50/200-day), golden and death crosses, RSI oversold or overbought, MACD crosses, Bollinger extremes, pullbacks to the 50-day, 52-week breakouts and unusual volume. Setup is 65+, Avoid is 35 or below. The suggested stop is two ATRs (average true range) below the price. Earnings within a week are flagged because results can gap a stock either way.

Fundamentals and prices from Yahoo Finance, refreshed after each US close and on Sunday. Sector norms differ, so treat value scores as a starting point for your own research. Not financial advice.
