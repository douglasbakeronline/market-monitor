Human-readable record of what changed and why. The weekly review deck is built from this file, the git log and the live data. Newest first. Bot data commits ("Update monitor data", "Update index scan") are not listed.

## Week of 2 November 2026

### Fri 6 Nov (midday)
- **Buy-tier and dip-reserve backtest** (roadmap 4): `tools/tier-backtest.mjs` (logic in `src/tierBacktest.js`, with tests) replays the buy tiers on S&P 500 daily data since 1928 (and the FTSE 100 since 1985) and simulates a monthly saver who keeps 15% in cash for confirmed tiers, against investing it all, over every 5, 10 and 20-year window, with total returns since 1988 and T-bill interest on cash. Result: the agreed reserve trails all-in by a median 1.2% of final wealth over 10 years and wins in only 8% of windows; a 10% reserve or the 6-month drip roughly halve that; fear confirmation makes little difference. Details in the research log. *Why:* calibrates the tiers and gives Douglas numbers for the open reserve-size decision. No rules changed.
- **Research page: quick scores show the full score from your list.** In the index scan, a quick-scored stock that is also on the research list now reads "Quick · list 84" (META, Shell, Constellation and others), and its detail says the full score and verdict. *Why:* META showed "Fair 75" in the scan but "Good entry 84" on the list, which could look like a contradiction; the pick check uses the full score.

### Thu 5 Nov (night)
- **Entry-pillar test on our own data.** `tools/entry-test.mjs` (logic in `src/entryTest.js`, with tests) samples every index stock at each month-end over ten years and compares 6 and 12-month returns, against the average stock that month, by distance from the 52-week high and by 12-1 month momentum. Result: stocks 10% to 30% off their high did no better than stocks near it (3 of 9 years ahead), while the strongest momentum fifth did best on average. Details and caveats (survivorship) in the research log. *Why:* roadmap item 3; the Entry pillar currently rewards exactly that 20% to 30% discount. No rule changes: the proposal waits on Douglas.
- **Peer view: valuation against the sector, display only.** The scan now works out each sector's median P/E (S&P 500 GICS, FTSE 100 ICB and Yahoo sector names mapped onto GICS's eleven; at least 8 peers; investment trusts left out) and stores it in `scan.json` (`sectorMedians`). Each stock, in the scan and on your list, gets a "Peer view" note on the Research page: its P/E against the sector median, a cyclical flag (Energy, Materials, chips and hardware, autos, miners, chemicals, housebuilders and similar) with a "Cyclical" tag, and the value score and verdict it would get if P/E were scored against peers with cyclicals capped at 60. Verdicts, alerts and the pick tranche check are unchanged. Logic in `src/sectorValue.js` and an optional third argument to `longTerm()`, with tests. *Why:* roadmap item 2 (research log 2 Nov): within-sector value predicts returns better than fixed bands, and a low P/E in a cyclical often marks peak earnings (Sandisk and Micron at P/E 3 to 4 top the scan). Showing the would-be verdicts first lets Douglas judge the change before it touches the plan. Tested on real data in a dry run: 7 of 109 full-score verdicts would change (CF, Super Micro and Glencore lose Good entry).

### Thu 5 Nov (evening)
- **Swing retune tested: no stricter setup clearly beats random entry.** `tools/swing-backtest.mjs` now replays four years (Nov 2022 to Nov 2026, about 15,700 setups on 598 index stocks) and tags each trade with its score, market, trend and whether results were near, and `src/swingRetune.js` compares eight variants (score 75+, uptrend only, US only, no results within 10 days, and combinations) with random entry under the same conditions, after Trading 212 costs (0.15% FX each way on US shares, 0.5% stamp duty on UK buys).
